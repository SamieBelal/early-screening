The earlier, the better.

If something about your child's development feels off, you don't need a diagnosis to get help.

Check how your child is doing

Two free tools, based on your child's age.

2 months to 5 years

CDC Milestone Tracker

A free app from the CDC. Check off milestones with photos and videos, and get a summary to show your child's doctor.

Get the free app
16 to 30 months

M-CHAT-R/F autism screening

The 20-question checklist pediatricians use at the 18- and 24-month checkups. It tells you whether it's worth a closer look. It isn't a diagnosis.

Ask your pediatrician to do it with you, or read it ahead of time on the official site.

Why early matters

A child's brain changes fastest in the first few years. Here's what the research shows.

  • In one trial, toddlers who started therapy at 18 to 30 months gained about 17.6 IQ points over two years, compared with 7 points with usual community care. Dawson et al., Pediatrics 2010. doi:10.1542/peds.2009-0958
  • Children diagnosed before age 2½ were three times more likely to show big improvements in social skills within one to two years. Gabbay-Dizdar et al., Autism 2022. doi:10.1177/13623613211049011
  • Babies showing early signs of autism whose parents got 10 coaching sessions were far less likely to meet criteria for autism at age 3: 6.7%, compared with 20.5% with usual care. Whitehouse et al., JAMA Pediatrics 2021. doi:10.1001/jamapediatrics.2021.3298

Starting later still helps. Starting earlier can help more.

This page is not medical advice and isn't a substitute for your child's doctor. Screening tools can miss things. If you're still worried after a normal result, trust your gut and talk to your pediatrician.
