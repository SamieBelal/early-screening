For parents

The earlier, the better.

If something about your child's development feels off, you don't have to wait for a diagnosis to get help. Here's where to start.

Is my child on track?

Two free tools, based on your child's age.

2 months – 5 years

CDC Milestone Tracker

A free app from the CDC. Check off milestones with photos and videos, and get a summary you can show your child's doctor.

Get the free app
16 – 30 months

M-CHAT-R/F autism screening

The 20-question checklist pediatricians use at the 18- and 24-month checkups. It's a screening, not a diagnosis. It tells you whether it's worth a closer look.

Ask your pediatrician to do it with you, or look at it ahead of time on the official site.

Official M-CHAT site

Worried? Here's what to do.

Do these at the same time, not one after another.

  1. Talk to your pediatrician.Tell them what you've noticed and ask for a developmental screening.
  2. Get on a waitlist now.Autism evaluations can take months to a year to get into. Your spot in line starts when you call.
  3. Start therapy while you wait.Speech therapy and occupational therapy (OT) don't require an autism diagnosis.
  4. Call Early Intervention (under 3).Every state has a free or low-cost program for babies and toddlers. You can call yourself. No referral or diagnosis needed.
  5. Age 3 or older? Call your school district.Your local public school district must evaluate children with possible disabilities for free, even if they aren't in school yet.

Why early matters

A child's brain changes fastest in the first few years. The research backs up starting early.

~Age 4

The typical age a child in the US is diagnosed with autism, often years after the first signs.

Shaw et al., MMWR 2025 · doi:10.15585/mmwr.ss7402a1
+17.6 vs +7

In one trial, toddlers who started therapy at 18–30 months gained about 17.6 IQ points over 2 years, compared with 7 points with usual community care.

Dawson et al., Pediatrics 2010 · doi:10.1542/peds.2009-0958
3×

Children diagnosed before age 2½ were three times more likely to show big improvements in social skills within 1–2 years.

Gabbay-Dizdar et al., Autism 2022 · doi:10.1177/13623613211049011

Starting later still helps. Starting earlier can help more.

This page is not medical advice and isn't a substitute for your child's doctor. Screening tools can miss things. If you're still worried after a normal result, trust your gut and talk to your pediatrician.
